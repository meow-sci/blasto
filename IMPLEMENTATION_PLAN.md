# KSA landing blast effects implementation plan

Reproduce the reference image with persistent, irregular radial scour marks blended into the planet's terrain material, then add dust emitted where exhaust strikes the surface. Start with Luna and Mars. The rendering design can also support Earth; convincing Earth behavior additionally requires surface classification and rules for vegetation, water and constructed pads.

The recommended first milestone is one manually placed lunar mark that receives native terrain lighting, remains stationary through camera and terrain LOD changes, and survives a save/load. The main engineering uncertainty is connecting a private terrain shader variant to dynamic mark data. Engine telemetry, surface sampling, body-fixed placement and particle infrastructure already have concrete source-level entry points.

Implementation status: the first standalone mod is now implemented. See [README.md](README.md) for the delivered scope and [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md) for automated results and pending in-game acceptance. This document preserves the original research and longer-term design; sparse texture tiles, weathering, excavation and rock sheltering remain future work. Research used three GPT-6.1-sol agents, the KSA modding corpus, Unscience, current decompiled assemblies and the Linux assets. Both local KSA binaries contain version `2026.10.10.5554`; the corpus targets that build. Source references below were checked against these local trees on October 11, 2026.

**The appearance to reproduce**

The reference's defining feature is a persistent ground pattern: uneven spokes, narrow dark streaks, broad softly disturbed areas, and intact terrain detail and rocks. A strong approximation should combine:

- A central disturbed patch, with strength and radius driven by engine exposure.
- Uneven radial fans and fine broken streaks, with a stable random seed per site.
- A faint outer deposition region and feathered edges, so the footprint has no visible rectangular boundary.
- Small changes to albedo and roughness, with optional shallow normal-map grooves. Preserve the original regolith texture underneath.
- Asymmetry from nozzle tilt, lateral motion, overlapping engines and local slopes.

Treat this as disturbed surface material. Darkening is an artistic parameter, not a rule that all engine exhaust produces black soot. Mars provides a useful physical reference: landing blasts can remove bright dust and expose a darker surface, and those patterns can change over time. A simple optional age fade would be an approximation, not a weather simulation. [NASA JPL observations](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia19159-changes-in-scars-from-2012-mars-landing/).

**Choose the terrain material route**

| Approach | Useful capability | Main limitation | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Modify `Planet.frag` before lighting | Terrain-only albedo, roughness and normal changes; native terrain lighting and shadows continue | Requires a new shader integration and dynamic GPU data binding | Production target |
| Adapt Graffiti's depth-projected decals | Existing projection, texture upload, anchors and persistence | Can paint vessels and clutter; its current lighting is independent of terrain lighting | Visual prototype or fallback |
| Rewrite terrain vertex colors | Existing public vertex/color buffers feed native lighting | Detail depends on mesh spacing and changes with LOD/material blending | Too coarse for the main effect |
| Use native terrain `Decal` modifiers | Height addition or flattening | These are height maps, with startup-sized resources; they do not paint local material properties | Separate future excavation feature |

KSA terrain is forward rendered and explicitly excluded from the depth/normal pre-pass. There is no ordinary terrain material G-buffer into which a deferred decal can simply be inserted. In `Planet.frag`, blend the mark after terrain material evaluation and before the PBR/Hapke lighting branches. This changes the material that existing sun, terrain, cloud and cascade shadow calculations illuminate. [Terrain rendering declaration](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/Shaders/Planet/Planet.frag:28), [material and lighting boundary](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/Shaders/Planet/Planet.frag:251), [shadow combination](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/Shaders/Planet/Planet.frag:296).

Graffiti is valuable reference code, but its terrain anchor only controls where its projection box sits. Its shader uses resolved scene depth with volume and facing tests, so landing legs and rocks inside the box can receive the image too. Its independent sun/ambient shading also bypasses the terrain material response. A proposed multiplicative HDR blend would preserve more existing shading for dark marks, but a reliable terrain receiver mask would still be needed. [Projection tests](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/graffiti.lib/DecalShaders.cs:78), [independent lighting](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/graffiti.lib/DecalShaders.cs:131).

The shipped `_Decals/circle.dds` and `Luna/TestDecalHeight.png` are not hidden scorch support. The former is used for launch-site levelling; the latter is referenced by `MarsTestDecal`. The GPU modifier edits height, not albedo. Physical craters would require terrain generation, collision and cache coordination beyond this effect. [Stock height decal](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/Astronomicals.xml:734), [height operation](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/Shaders/Planet/ProceduralModifiersLibrary/Modifiers.glsl:481).

**Build the renderer integration first**

Use a mod-owned shader variant and resources; preserve the installed asset files. Two seams are plausible: intercept the initial `PlanetFrag` compilation early enough, or explicitly compile and substitute a private stage when `PlanetRenderer.BuildShaderStages` runs. Humble Arteest demonstrates preserving compile options and include paths during shader source substitution. Its part-specific activation sequence is not directly reusable: the planet renderer reads cached `ShaderReference.Shader` modules, and rebuilding its pipeline alone does not recompile them. [Unscience interception](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/humble-arteest.lib/VehiclePaintPatches.cs:136), [planet stage selection](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/PlanetRenderer.cs:1687), [cached module use](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/RenderCore/RenderTechnique.cs:35).

Start with one procedural mark, then transport its parameters through a private GPU storage buffer. KSA already uses six descriptor sets and a 128-byte push-constant block for terrain. Adding a shader declaration alone is insufficient. The prototype must construct a compatible pipeline layout, bind the mark buffer for each frame/view, and verify device limits. Prefer extending the renderer-owned set 2 after auditing its shared consumers: bindings 0 through 12 are occupied, so a new binding also requires matching descriptor pool sizing, allocation and writes. A seventh set is an alternative only where the device supports it. Do not overwrite occupied bindings or assume spare push constants. Finalize this choice in the prototype. [Existing bindings](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/PlanetRenderer.cs:509), [pipeline layout](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/PlanetRenderer.cs:612), [push constants](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA.Rendering.Planet/PlanetViewportData.cs:33).

Sphinx is the closest Unscience model for aligned per-frame/per-view buffers and descriptors. Graffiti supplies texture upload, image transition and bindless sampling patterns. Neither already binds data into the terrain shader. Create replacement stages/layout/resources as a consistent set; if validation or compilation fails, retain the stock renderer. Release graphics resources before renderer/device destruction and make later unload cleanup idempotent. [Sphinx buffers](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/sphinx.lib/StaticModelResources.cs:44), [Graffiti renderer](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/graffiti.lib/DecalRenderer.cs:97).

Store each site in double-precision body-fixed coordinates, with a tangent frame and stable body ID. Convert its center and axes into camera-relative coordinates each frame, then pack floats. In the fragment shader, subtract the nearby mark center from the nearby fragment position and project that small difference onto the tangent axes. Avoid subtracting planet-sized float coordinates; the game's precision library documents Earth-scale quantization problems. Reuse Graffiti's body-fixed anchor calculations, not its projection depth heuristics. [Anchors](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/graffiti.lib/DecalAnchors.cs:76), [precision constraints](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/Shaders/Common/PrecisionFuncs.glsl:7).

A tessellation wrinkle needs an early visual test: the current evaluation shader displaces `gl_Position` but passes the undisplaced position to the fragment shader. If fine marks swim or misalign with relief, provide a dedicated displaced position varying for mark lookup rather than casually changing every lighting consumer. [Tessellation outputs](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/Shaders/Planet/PlanetTessEvaluation.tese:156).

**Generate marks from engine exposure**

Use every relevant simulated nozzle on nearby vehicles, including uncontrolled vehicles. Do not place one decal at the vessel's center or trigger only on touchdown: hovering and launch burns should also disturb the ground.

1. Sample actual nozzle output and gimballed geometry. `RocketNozzleState` exposes `Performance.TotalThrust`, thrust and visual exhaust locations/directions, `ThrustTime`, exhaust conditions and exhaust velocity. Use the physical thrust geometry to estimate impingement, with the visual nozzle position as an alignment check. Exhaust points opposite the thrust direction. [State](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/RocketNozzleState.cs:9), [state update and gimbal transforms](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/RocketNozzle.cs:291).
2. Transform the nozzle and ray into the parent body's fixed frame in double precision. Broad-phase reject distant engines and rays that cannot meet the surface. Find the first positive ray/height intersection using bounded stepping and bracketed refinement; include a few cone samples where an inclined plume grazes terrain. Derive a terrain normal from nearby accurate height samples. Cache local samples and stagger queries across active nozzles.
3. Accumulate a surface exposure field using actual thrust, distance, incidence angle, duration and a tunable surface response. A useful visual model is `D += k * max(T * incidence / footprintArea - threshold, 0) * dtBurn`, followed by `strength = 1 - exp(-D / saturationDose)`. This is a calibrated graphics model, not a physical shear-stress solver. Bound the near-ground footprint, use smooth altitude falloff and let multiple nozzles contribute to the same field.
4. Paint stable radial and anisotropic kernels along the moving impact point. Deposit into persistent site tiles so repeated burns saturate and moving landers leave a continuous history. Separate accumulated exposure from fine artistic streak detail; the latter should not flicker or rotate randomly each update.

Enumerate `vehicle.Parts.RocketNozzles.ModulesAndStates`. For main-thread applied poses, `vehicle.PosAsmbToBody` supplies the center-of-mass correction, followed by `Body2Cce`, `GetPositionCce()` and the parent body's `GetCce2Ccf()` rotation. The nozzle state already contains gimbal rotation; do not apply it twice. Native exhaust rendering supplies the transform example. [Nozzle collection](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/PartTree.cs:76), [native exhaust transforms](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/Vehicle.cs:5747).

`Celestial.GetTerrainHeightFromDirCcf(normalizedDirection, accurate: true)` gives radial height including procedural modifiers. The ray solver seeks the first crossing of `length(point) - MeanRadius - terrainHeight(pointDirection)`. The native `TerrainImpactFinder` is trajectory-oriented and samples with `accurate:false`, so it is not an exact drop-in for this purpose. CPU height still needs visual comparison against tessellated terrain detail. [Accurate height API](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/Celestial.cs:789), [native impact clearance](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/TerrainImpactFinder.cs:64).

Actual performance already incorporates engine state and ambient conditions. Do not multiply it by commanded throttle again. `AverageThrustFraction` and `DutyCycle` summarize chamber-pressure activity, not integrated thrust. For a normal-speed prototype, sample once per applied simulation step. For robust cutoffs, pulsed engines and timewarp, capture copied nozzle/pose samples at the physics substep and integrate thrust over the actual burn interval. Queue immutable samples and consume them after the corresponding solver step is applied; do not access the renderer from physics workers. [Statistics implementation](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/RocketNozzleState.cs:61).

Candidate capture points are `VehicleUpdateState.UpdateModules(SimStep)` and `AccumulateRocketNozzleStepData(float dt)`, with exact pre/post-step ordering to validate. Worker `ReadOnlyPhysicsStates.GetStatesCcf` supplies a consistent pose. Drain completed samples after `Universe.ApplyVehicleSolvers`; use the native `ActiveNozzle.ComputeThrustMod` burn-time clipping rule rather than inferring impulse from frame-end throttle. [Worker module update](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/VehicleUpdateState.cs:381), [worker pose](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/ReadOnlyPhysicsStates.cs:68), [burn clipping](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/ActiveNozzle.cs:19), [apply boundary](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/Universe.cs:1947).

Consume each simulation interval once, independently of render frame count or number of viewports. Pause adds no exposure. In unsupported large warp steps, preserve existing marks and suspend generation explicitly until substep capture is supported; do not turn a single endpoint into an enormous burn. UI visibility must not control effect updates.

For fidelity, use several authored or procedural streak variants, rotated by the site's stable seed, with multiscale noise and mipmaps. Bias rays with plume direction and motion. Broad slopes can shape the footprint using height samples. Small rock sheltering, excavation and clutter removal are separate later features; terrain-only shading leaves current rock geometry intact. That already reproduces much of the reference, but not every rock's downstream protected streak.

**Use sparse local data**

Begin with a small bounded list of procedural marks to prove rendering. For repeated landings, accumulate into sparse body-fixed surface tiles and merge nearby exposures. Keep a spatial index by body and location, upload only visible nearby tiles, and avoid a per-pixel loop over the entire saved history. Large footprints should span several local charts rather than stretch a single tangent plane over significant curvature.

As a starting budget to measure, a 512 by 512 two-channel 16-bit field costs 1 MiB without mipmaps, about 1.33 MiB with a full mip chain. Eight resident tiles would therefore cost about 10.7 MiB for this field alone, excluding staging, metadata, art textures and any double buffering. Choose tile size from the largest tuned footprint and desired ground resolution; these numbers are design inputs, not measured performance.

Use exposure/deposition fields for durable state and a separate detail atlas or procedural layer for crisp streaks. Rebuild mipmaps after changes and update dirty regions. Keep CPU persistence data or a deterministic bounded representation; never depend on reading back GPU state during a scene teardown. Evicting a tile from GPU memory must not erase a saved landing site.

`PlanetRenderer` and `DistantSphereRenderer` are separate paths. A terrain-only shader patch does not automatically add marks to distant planetary spheres. Cull subpixel footprints and retain them for the next approach. Giant scars visibly resolvable from orbit would require an additional distant-rendering path. [Nearby terrain selection](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/PlanetRenderer.cs:1960), [distant sphere material](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/DistantSphereRenderer.cs:88).

**Add landing dust after the ground marks work**

The native particle system can acquire mod-defined XML emitters; KSArmory provides the corpus example. Stock `PlanetImpact` includes debris and a volumetric dust-like child, making it useful reference material. Author dedicated landing emitters rather than triggering full impacts every frame. [Particle acquisition](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA.Rendering.Particles/ParticleSystem.cs:259), [stock effect](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/ParticleEmitterAssets.xml:247).

Emit at surface impact locations, with velocity mostly along the local tangent plane and a controlled upward component. Drive emission from new exposure/erosion, not the accumulated darkness. Keep detached dust's `Context.Vehicle` null so it remains anchored to the surface. The public `ParticleSpawnLogic` delegate permits custom initial positions/velocities; current CCF spawning also rotates them by `LocalOffset`. This corrects the corpus's broader warning that a body-fixed emitter cannot be aimed: its GPU offset is identity, but its spawn-time rotation is present in this build. [Delegate](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA.Rendering.Particles/ParticleEmitter.cs:21), [CCF spawn rotation](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA.Rendering.Particles/ParticleEmitter.cs:608).

Reuse the native gravity and atmosphere response as a starting point. It derives drag from local density and treats pressure below 100 Pa as vacuum. Tune particle size, opacity, lifetime and velocities per surface/body. Avoid treating screen-space particle collision as authoritative ground collision, since it depends on the current view. Cap pooled emitters and particles; handle acquisition failure by reducing dust, without losing persistent exposure. Stop endless emitters with `Kill()` before removing ownership, and clear them on scene changes. [Atmosphere response](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA.Rendering.Particles/ParticleEmitter.cs:471), [movement](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/Shaders/Particles/Update/SimpleMovement.comp:36).

| Surface | Proposed mark treatment | Proposed dust treatment | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Luna | Subtle gray regolith disturbance, long irregular rays, persistent site | Low, fast outward sheets and ballistic grains; little lingering haze | First target |
| Mars | Reddish dust removal with darker exposed material where appropriate | Denser dust, some atmospheric drag; optional slow age fade | Second target |
| Earth soil or sand | Exposed soil and material-dependent color change | Dragged and lofted dust with shorter horizontal travel | Same renderer, additional profiles |
| Earth rock or concrete | Weaker surface disturbance, optional authored discoloration | Little loose material | Receiver classification needed |
| Snow or ice | Surface-specific pale disturbance | Different particle appearance | Later profile |
| Water | No persistent regolith mark on the seabed from a surface interaction | Optional spray | Explicit exclusion initially |
| Vegetated ground | Ground mark can render beneath existing vegetation | Dust where appropriate | Vegetation removal/flattening is separate work |

The game exposes `SampleBiomeIdMap` and `SampleBiomeControlMap`, plus biome ground/slope materials. Use those to select a mod-owned response table: the native fields describe appearance, not soil cohesion or erosion thresholds. Read atmospheric pressure/density at runtime, allowing modified celestial systems. The stock lunar body ID is `Luna`. [Biome samples](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/Celestial.cs:1245), [material references](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/BiomeMaterialReference.cs:8), [body IDs](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-linux/Content/Core/SolSystem.xml:39).

Suppress ground erosion when the local ocean radius (`MeanRadius + OceanReference.Level`) is above terrain. A ray against terrain height does not detect an intervening pad, building or vehicle. Matching those occluders to collision/render geometry is a separate unresolved query; initially use explicit pad exclusions or authored response regions, and test unobstructed dry ground. Do not claim generic Earth support merely because a lunar mark can be drawn there. [Ocean radius](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/ksa-game-assemblies/current/decomp/KSA/PhysicsEnvironment.cs:127).

These are artistic behavior targets informed by pressure/gravity differences, not validated plume-flow predictions. NASA's plume/surface work treats gas and regolith as a complex multiphase flow; a practical game mod should use exposure kernels and particles rather than attempt that solver. [NASA plume interaction modeling](https://www.nas.nasa.gov/SC22/research/project12.html).

**Keep sites independent of vehicles**

Build this as a focused StarMap mod in `poc-blasto`, with the rendering and exposure logic in small independent components. Suggested responsibilities are `NozzleSampler`, `SurfaceProbe`, `BlastAccumulator`, `SurfaceProfiles`, `BlastSiteStore`, `TerrainBlastRenderer`, `LandingDust` and `BlastPersistence`. Those are proposed module names, not existing APIs.

Save the exact body ID, double-precision anchor and tangent orientation, extent, exposure/deposition data, stable seed, creation time, profile/art version and schema version. The mark must survive deletion or departure of its lander. Persist the accumulated field or sufficient deterministic data to rebuild it; a seed and one strength value cannot reproduce an arbitrary moving multi-engine history. Airborne particles remain transient.

Graffiti's save adapter is the closest model for detached capture, validation, clearing the old world and replay after reconstruction. For a standalone mod, use an independently owned `blasto.json` sidecar and save hooks. Unscience's shared hooks have single-host settable callbacks and its storage hardcodes `unscience.json`; attaching a second coordinator would conflict. If integrated into Unscience later, register through its existing `ISaveParticipantSource` coordinator instead. [Graffiti save adapter](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/graffiti.lib/GraffitiSubmod.Saves.cs:13), [callback ownership](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/ksa-abstractions.lib/Persistence/NativeSaveHooks.cs:18), [sidecar storage](/Users/asherwin/repos/meow-sci/unscience/ksa-abstractions.lib/Persistence/SaveStorage.cs:12).

Validate body resolution exactly; never redirect unresolved marks to the current body. Bind sidecars to their native save, replace them atomically, support legacy/missing data, and reset state when loading a vanilla save. Do not serialize native objects, shader modules, pointers or GPU handles.

**Implementation sequence and acceptance gates**

1. **Prove one terrain mark.** Compile a private terrain variant and bind one dynamic mark. Its position and strength must update without shader recompilation or Vulkan validation errors. Use a debug footprint first, then a radial material mask. It must shade with the terrain, leave the craft unpainted, remain stable through camera motion and tessellation changes, and restore stock rendering when disabled. This is the architecture gate; stop expanding scope until it passes.
2. **Make the lunar result convincing.** Add material-preserving streak art, feathering, roughness and restrained normal detail. Compare fixed screenshots in full sun, grazing light and a lander shadow, from ground and overhead views. Add body-fixed persistence, depart/return and repeated save/load tests.
3. **Connect real engines.** Validate one nozzle, multiple engines, gimbal, tilted craft, hover, descent, ascent, cutoff, fuel exhaustion and uncontrolled nearby craft. Verify no marks from upward/high-altitude plumes and identical exposure at different render frame rates. Add immutable substep capture for burn timing and warp behavior.
4. **Add dust and Mars.** Use bounded native emitters and custom ground-relative velocities. Test pause, warp transitions, particle setting off, exhausted pools, camera changes and scene teardown. Tune Martian appearance independently of Luna.
5. **Scale and broaden surfaces.** Add sparse tiles, spatial culling, dirty updates and resident budgets. Measure CPU/GPU frame cost and VRAM with many saved sites and overlapping active engines. Add Earth dry-surface rules, then decide separately whether water spray, vegetation or physical excavation is worth the extra work.

Across all phases, exercise resolution/MSAA changes, renderer rebuilds, multiple viewports, latitude/longitude seams, poles, steep slopes and different body rotations. Check GPU resources are released before device teardown. Managed checks should cover coordinate transforms, ray/height intersection edge cases, time/dose accumulation and real persistence round trips; native game captures and profiling are required for rendering acceptance.

The most consequential unknowns are the dynamic planet pipeline integration, sub-meter stability with tessellation/LOD, and a reliable Earth surface classifier. The first milestone tests the first two directly. Once it succeeds, automatic footprint creation and dust can be added without changing the core visual approach.
